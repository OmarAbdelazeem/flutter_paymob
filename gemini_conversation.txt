

Can I use paymob inside my flutter app without backend ?

Technically, yes, you can use Paymob in your Flutter app without a custom backend by handling all the API calls (Authentication, Order Registration, and Payment Key generation) directly within your Dart code.
However, just because you can doesn't always mean you should. Here is the breakdown of how it works and why you need to be careful.
1. How to do it (The "No-Backend" Way)
When you don't have a backend, your Flutter app must perform the 3-step Paymob handshake itself using a package like http or a dedicated Paymob wrapper like flutter_paymob or pay_with_paymob.
The Workflow:
1. Authentication: Send your api_key to Paymob to get an auth_token.
2. Order Registration: Use the auth_token and cart details to create an order_id.
3. Payment Key: Send the order_id and your integration_id to get a payment_key.
4. Display IFrame: Open a WebView (IFrame) using that key so the user can enter card details.

2. The Risks of This Approach
While this is great for prototyping or small personal projects, there are two major "Red Flags":
* Security (The Biggest Issue): To get the auth_token, you have to hardcode your API Secret Key inside your Flutter app. A smart user could "decompile" your APK, steal your key, and potentially access your Paymob dashboard or perform unauthorized actions.
* Transaction Verification: Without a backend, you are relying on the Flutter app to "tell" you the payment was successful. If a user’s internet cuts out right after they pay—or if they manually trigger the "Success" UI without paying—your app might grant them access to a product they didn't actually buy.

Ok is WebView (IFrame) the only way for using paymob ?

No, a WebView is not your only option. While it’s the most common way to handle credit card payments, Paymob offers a few different paths depending on the "vibe" and complexity you want for your Flutter app.
Here are the three main ways to handle the payment interface:
1. The WebView (Standard IFrame)
This is what most people use. You get a URL from the Payment Key step and load it in a package like webview_flutter.
* Pros: Easiest to implement; Paymob handles the security (PCI compliance) entirely.
* Cons: Feels a bit like a "website inside an app," which can break the native feel.
2. Native SDKs (The "Pro" Way)
Paymob provides a Native Android and iOS SDK. While they don't have an "official" Flutter plugin that is updated as frequently as their mobile ones, you can use Method Channels to bridge the native SDKs into your Flutter app.


* How it works: You write a bit of Kotlin (Android) and Swift (iOS) code to trigger Paymob’s native UI components.
* Pros: Much smoother animations, better user experience, and feels like a natural part of your app.
* Cons: Requires more advanced coding knowledge to bridge the native code to Flutter.
3. Mobile Wallet Integration (Intent-based)
If you are specifically targeting users in markets like Egypt (where Vodafone Cash, InstaPay, or Meeza are huge), you don't necessarily need a WebView for the whole process.
* How it works: You generate a "Redirection URL" for the wallet. When the user clicks pay, it can trigger an "Intent" to open the specific wallet app on their phone or a simple input field for their phone number.
* Pros: Extremely high conversion rates for local markets.

Ok what if used Native SDKs with backend what will be the flow ?

Using the Native SDK along with a Backend is the "Gold Standard" for security and user experience. In this setup, your app never touches the sensitive API Secret Keys, and your backend acts as the secure gatekeeper.
Here is exactly how that flow looks:
The "Pro" Architecture Flow
1. Handshake (App ↔ Backend): Your Flutter app sends the cart details (items, price) to your backend.
2. Secure Authentication (Backend ↔ Paymob): * Your backend sends your API_KEY to Paymob to get an auth_token.
    * It then creates an Order to get an order_id.
    * Finally, it requests a payment_key.
3. The Handoff (Backend ↔ App): Your backend sends the payment_key back to the Flutter app.
4. Native UI (App ↔ User): * The Flutter app passes this payment_key into the Native SDK.
    * The SDK opens a smooth, native credit card entry form (no browser look-and-feel).
5. Final Verification (Paymob ↔ Backend): * Once the user pays, Paymob sends a Webhook (a POST request) directly to your backend.
    * Your backend verifies the payment and updates your database (e.g., "Order #123 is now Paid").

Implementation Tips for Flutter
Since Paymob’s official Native SDKs are written in Kotlin (Android) and Swift (iOS), you have two choices for implementation:
* Method Channels: You can write a small "bridge" yourself. Your Flutter code calls platform.invokeMethod('startPayment', paymentKey), and you write the native code to launch the Paymob SDK activity/view.
* The "Intention" API: Paymob has recently pushed their "Intention API" which simplifies this. Your backend creates an "Intention," and the SDK handles the rest of the flow with even fewer steps.
Why this is better
By using the backend, if the user's phone dies exactly 2 seconds after they hit "Pay," your backend will still receive the Webhook from Paymob. Your database will be updated, and the user will find their item waiting for them when they recharge their phone.
